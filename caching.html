Prompt caching only pays off if your calls land close enough together.

Caching means the model stores a prompt so it does not have to re-read the whole thing next time. The first call writes the cache, and that costs extra. Every call after that reads the cache, and that costs less than a normal input. But if the cache expires before your next call shows up, you paid the write fee for nothing. Slide your own numbers and see which side you land on.

Three traps

1. The slow bot trap If you wait too long between calls, the cache expires before you come back. Every call pays the write fee and none of them get the cheap read price. Check yourself: is your gap between calls, set above, bigger than your cache window? If yes, you are in this trap right now.
2. The storage rent trap Some caches, like Gemini's, charge rent for every hour you hold them, whether you read them again or not. Check yourself: multiply hours held by the storage rate. If that number is bigger than what the cheap reads actually saved you, you are paying to store something you are not using.
3. Context creep trap In a growing chat, the whole conversation so far has to be re-cached and re-read on every single turn. The prefix gets bigger each turn, so the bill per turn quietly climbs even though nothing about your usage pattern changed. Check yourself: track your cost per turn over a long chat. If it is rising, this is why.
